What 18 years of Toronto planning files say, once the text is parsed

The city's open dataset lists every planning application, but the facts that matter (units, height, density, parking) only appear in a free-text description. I parsed those descriptions into columns and ran some standard text analysis over them. This is a first pass with regex rules, no LLM yet.

Rezoning & Official Plan Amendment (OZ) files

Proposals got taller and more numerous after 2019

Units proposed in rezoning applications jumped in 2020–22, and the median proposal height more than doubled. Since 2024, the typical rezoning asks for a 35+ storey building.

Ontario Land Tribunal

Taller proposals end up at the tribunal more often

Share of 2016–2022 rezoning proposals whose current status shows a tribunal appeal. Towers of 31+ storeys are appealed at roughly 2.5 times the rate of mid-rise.

The status field overwrites history

An application that went to the tribunal and later closed just reads "Closed". That's why pre-2016 appeal rates look near zero, and why submission year alone predicts an appeal (AUC 0.76) about as well as anything in the project itself. Real outcome labels need decision dates from another source.

How the language shifted

Terms most over-represented in descriptions from 2021 onward versus 2008–2013. Part of this is real (mixed-use towers replacing single houses and additions) and part is the city's writing template changing (GFA reporting became standard).

Baseline classifier

Can the description predict an appeal?

Logistic regression, 5-fold cross-validation, OZ proposals 2008–2022 ( files, appealed). Outcome words like "appeal" and "OLT" are removed from the text so the model can't cheat.

Text carries some signal, but most of the structured model's edge comes from submission year, which mostly reflects the status-overwrite problem above. Treat this as a pipeline test, not a finding.

Extraction quality

What the rules caught

Coverage on descriptions that look residential. A spot check of 20 random unit counts found 1 error (commercial units counted as homes). About 2,000 ambiguous descriptions are queued for an overnight local-LLM pass.
